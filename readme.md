3. Introduction: Automated modulation classification (AMC) is a key technology in the sixth-generation (6G) network spectrum perception [1][2]. Although deep learning (DL)-based AMC methods have achieved significant results, these methods rely on massive datasets, which perform poorly in unknown channel environments [3]. To address this issue, during training, it is necessary to configure a labeled dataset containing new transmitter parameters and channel conditions to ensure the model adapts to new environments. However, acquiring sufficient labeled data in non-cooperative communication scenarios presents significant challenges. Therefore, research focuses have shifted towards cross-environment model transfer and maximizing the use of limited labeled data to solve this problem.
In recent years, transfer learning (TL) has emerged as a novel method for cross-environment knowledge transfer. Perenda et al. [3] incorporated transfer learning with spatial Transformer networks (STN) into the modulation classification based on ResNeXt to ensure model robustness under unknown shaping parameters (upsampling factors, pulse shaping filter parameters, and sampling frequencies) and channel models (additive Gaussian noise, Rayleigh, Rician). Zhang et al. [4] focused on leveraging transfer learning to enhance the generalization capability of models in unfamiliar modulation scheme environments, proposing an open set domain adaptive AMC (OSDA-AMC) algorithm that combines supervised adversarial domain adaptation technology with open set modulation recognition (OSMR). Zhou et al. [5] and Liang et al. [6] achieved knowledge transfer between different AMC datasets: [5] reusing pre-trained networks on large-scale datasets; [6] proposed an improved domain adversarial neural network with self-attention mechanism to preserve the domain-specific features of both target and source domains. However, most research related to transfer learning in the AMC field lacks exploration of cross-channel scenarios, while in practical applications, the distribution of channel environments significantly differs from training scenarios.
Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL) is another paradigm of learning that combines labeled and unlabeled data to train machine learning models, reducing the burden of data labeling. It is suitable for scenarios where labeled data is scarce. Liu et al. [7] proposed a pretraining method based on self-supervised contrastive learning, which uses a large amount of unlabeled data for pretraining and then fine-tunes downstream tasks with a small amount of labeled data. On this basis, Bai et al. [8] considered both modality-level and instance-level feature representations during the pretraining process of unlabeled data, extracting more robust features. These two methods essentially only train on the target domain, failing to leverage the correlation between different channel environments. Guo et al. [9] proposed the SemiAMR framework, using teacher-student models and pseudo-label training methods to address the issue of insufficient labeled data. Although semi-supervised learning in the AMC field has made some progress, most existing research assumes that the training set and test set distributions are consistent, while the differences in data distributions in real-world scenarios pose new challenges to research in this field.
In summary, current semi-supervised learning methods for AMC assume that the training set and test set distributions are identical. However, studies based on transfer learning can achieve knowledge transfer between datasets with different distributions, but few have focused on cross-channel scenarios. Therefore, Semi-Supervised Domain Adaptation (SSDA) is considered as a viable solution.
In machine learning, a "domain" refers to the knowledge domain applied by a specific machine learning model; domain shift refers to the statistical characteristics of data distribution during the training phase and deployment phase. Different channel environments correspond to different domains, leading to domain shifts. Domain adaptation (DA) is a machine learning paradigm aimed at solving the problem of knowledge transfer from the source domain (rich in labeled data and easily accessible) to the target domain (poor in labeled data or non-existent). In cross-channel scenarios, the AMC dataset sampled from the known training channel environment is labeled, which can be considered as the source domain; the AMC dataset sampled from the actual test channel environment lacks sufficient labeled data, which can be seen as the target domain. As shown in Figure 1, the distribution shift in wireless channels leads to domain shifts, and domain adaptation can find a distribution that effectively classifies both source and target domain modulation schemes. Semi-Supervised Domain Adaptation (SSDA) utilizes insufficient labeled data in the target domain, combining domain adaptation with semi-supervised learning.
